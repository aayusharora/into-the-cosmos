Lesson 6, as one query

Was it right?

SELECT

  COUNT(*) AS nights,

  SUM(said_go) AS told_them_to_go,

  SAFE_DIVIDE(

    SUM(said_go AND was_clear),

    SUM(said_go)) AS right_when_it_said_yes

FROM nights;

The answer turns up for free. Tomorrow.

Don't read the SQL. Say what it does: of all the nights it told somebody to go outside, on how many was the sky actually clear? Then the thing that makes this domain lucky, and point at it: the ground truth turns up on its own. Tomorrow morning, open-meteo publishes what the cloud cover actually was last night. You don't have to label anything. You just have to write down what you said, and check. That's the whole habit. One row per night: what it advised, and what happened. One query. Run it weekly. And the consequence is human, not technical. Send somebody out into cloud twice and they stop opening your app. Nothing in your logs tells you that happened. Finish on the bottom line and make it theirs, because it's the most transferable sentence in the talk. Everybody here already has ground truth and hasn't noticed. Closed tickets. Merged pull requests. Last quarter's forecasts against what actually happened. You already know how all of it turned out. Hold some back and score against it.
