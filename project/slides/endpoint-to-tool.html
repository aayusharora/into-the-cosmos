This is the slide people will photograph, and it's the one I kept forgetting to write. Everybody can call an API. The question nobody answers is what happens between the response and the model, and the answer is that you do it. Walk the four rows. NASA sends two days of hourly readings because that's what the endpoint does. Twelve hundred bytes, forty-eight numbers, almost none of them about tonight. Your function throws most of it away and keeps six hours and an average. The docstring tells the model what the numbers mean, including where the threshold is. And then the model sees a hundred and thirty-three bytes it can actually reason about. Say the ratio out loud: ten times smaller. And then the rule you can take away: never hand a model a raw API response. It will cost you tokens, it will slow you down, and it will find something in there to be confused by. Shape it first. That's your job. Every tool in this talk is these four steps and nothing else.
