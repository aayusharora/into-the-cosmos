This is lesson four turned into shape. Walk the two lanes, don't talk about syntax. Top lane runs once, at sunset, and nobody is waiting for it. A Cloud Run Job works through every city you care about. Seven seconds each, hundreds at a time, because none of them depends on the others. It writes one row per city per night into BigQuery. Bottom lane is what happens when somebody actually asks. The agent reads one row. Forty milliseconds instead of seven seconds. Then the line at the bottom, which is the whole idea and isn't really about the cloud at all: the sky over this city tonight is the same for everybody in this city tonight. There's no reason to compute it per question. Same container image, two entry points. That's the trick, and it's the one that makes a slow tool survive contact with real users. Say honestly that this is architecture. The gcloud commands are in the repo, and I've never run them, because there's no billing account behind this talk.
