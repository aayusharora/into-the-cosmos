One at a time. By number ten, the question answers itself.

This is the promise, and the rest of the first half keeps it. Read the headline, then don't read all ten out. Nobody remembers a list of ten read out loud. Instead: say there are ten, point at number one and number ten, and tell them the counter in the corner of every slide from here will tell them where they are. Then the bottom line, which is the contract: we add them one at a time, and when the tenth is in, the question answers itself. No slide after this introduces a concept because a textbook puts it next. Each one turns up because the question won't work without it. If you're running short this is the slide to linger on and the individual code slides to speed through. People remember the shape.
