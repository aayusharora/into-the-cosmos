The whole talk is one question

“Is it worth going outside tonight?”

✗It has no idea what tonight’s sky is doing.
✗It doesn’t know what city you’re standing in.
✗It doesn’t know you only own binoculars.
✗It can’t look anything up.
✗And it forgets all of this by tomorrow.
Ten things are missing. We’ll add them one at a time.

By the end it answers this by itself, every morning, before you’re awake.

This is the spine of the whole talk, so set it up properly and don't rush the list. Say the question out loud like a person, not a demo prompt. It's the most ordinary question in the world and a language model can't answer it. Not because it's stupid. Five separate things are missing, and by the end of the talk you'll have counted ten. Let the red lines land one at a time. Students recognise every single one of these failures; they have all asked a chatbot something it couldn't know. Then make the promise on the bottom line and mean it, because the talk delivers it on rung 9: the same question, answered automatically, in your inbox at six in the morning. Promising the ending here is what makes the middle feel like one thing instead of six demos. Come back to this question explicitly on rungs 3, 8 and 9.
